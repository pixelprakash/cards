Design as inquiry

Pedagogy Playcards is a card-based speculative tool for exploring alternative futures in higher education. With global challenges accelerating, there is a critical need to re/imagine learning frameworks that move beyond incremental reform. The tool works as a mechanism to generate futures, and a future requires what does not yet exist.

By assembling combinations of places, values, actions and modes, supported by prompts, participants construct possibility spaces where the educational status quo can be questioned and reconfigured. Design becomes inquiry: imagining alternative futures is a method for critical reflection and narrative construction, not a thought exercise.

Games function as social systems, representing reality and constructing social structures at the same time. The concept draws on Johan Huizinga’s Homo Ludens (1938), which frames humans as inherently playful beings.

Combine on the drafting template.

By combining the elements on a drafting template, participants generate draft narratives that explore alternative futures. Prompts turn the combination into a concise, vivid description of a future learning environment.

The outcome is a draft scenario, expected to act as a precursor to scenario building. Card play operates at the intersection of freedom and order: moments of spontaneity coexist with an underlying structure, and implicit rules guide play.

Nineteen students. Three teachers. One rough draft, played in Delhi.

Since that first session the game has been played in many situations, contexts and age groups, and the card numbers have changed several times. Despite its unstructured appearance it follows an internal logic.

Early drafts were often funny. Repeated iterations got more imaginative, and more meaningful. What the sessions taught: a suspension of disbelief is essential for participants to engage fully with speculative combinations and push the boundaries of what learning could become.

Themes the exercise gave rise to, among others

  • Anti-disciplinary knowledge clusters
  • Quantum pedagogy
  • Failure-Based Learning Models

New design-based directions in education that remain inaccessible when educational research stays rooted in the present.

The same inquiry, aimed at the thesis.

Temporal, multimodal, and open: Research assessment in the Age of AI, by the same authors, explores additions and alternatives to the conventional text-based thesis, now that AI writing tools are active participants in knowledge production. Six directions:

  • Beyond text

    Video, visualisation, simulation and audio as representations of critical thinking, tracking the research journey rather than only the hardbound result.

  • Multimodal and longitudinal

    Theses developed transparently over several semesters, so bias, contradiction and compromise become visible. Value moves from the polished outcome to the iteration.

  • Wiki-theses

    Students build, revise and present research together on public platforms, an evolving portfolio of contributions, peer feedback and ideas.

  • Failure-first research

    A thesis that documents dead ends and unviable hypotheses. When AI can generate flawless text, resilience becomes the evidence.

  • Embodied research outputs

    In disciplines intersecting with human behaviour, performative theses can replace text: live enactments, immersive experiences or sensory installations that engage evaluators beyond intellectual cognition.

  • Algorithmically co-authored

    In fields where machine-generated insights drive research, crediting AI as a co-author pushes the boundaries of accountability and intellectual labour. In Sarkar and Kotler’s Wicked Problems (March 2025), ClimateGPT is credited as co-author on the cover, and each chapter acknowledges the collaboration.

The caveat is institutional: standardisation, comparability, faculty readiness, resources, and how to assess non-textual work. Rigour still matters. So does adaptability.
